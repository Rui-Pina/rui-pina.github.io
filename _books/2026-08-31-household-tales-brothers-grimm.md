The Grimms collected stories in which hunger, cruelty and danger sit close to ordinary family life. Parents abandon children, strangers offer dangerous bargains, and the weak survive through luck or cunning. The bluntness gives even the familiar tales an edge that adaptations often remove. There is humour here too, especially when a fool gets the better of someone who considers himself clever.

Reading the collection together makes the recurring plots noticeable: impossible tasks, jealous siblings, rewards for kindness and savage punishments. Some tales feel like variations on ones already told. Even so, the range of strange encounters and memorable images makes this a rewarding collection, best read a few stories at a time.
