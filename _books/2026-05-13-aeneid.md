The Aeneid is Virgil’s enduring epic of duty, destiny, and the founding of Rome. Following the Trojan hero Aeneas on his journey across the Mediterranean, the poem combines stirring adventure with profound reflections on sacrifice, leadership, and the tension between personal desire and public responsibility.

Virgil’s poetry is rich in imagery and carefully structured, weaving together memorable episodes with themes that have shaped Western literature for centuries. The work rewards close reading, particularly for those interested in classical mythology, Roman history, or the development of the epic tradition. Depending on the translation, however, the language can be demanding, and the poem’s frequent references to mythological and historical figures may require additional context for first-time readers.
