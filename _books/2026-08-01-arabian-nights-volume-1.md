Shahrazad tells stories to postpone her execution, and that danger gives the whole collection its urgency. Within her tales, characters begin stories of their own, drawing the reader through accounts of merchants and rulers, desire and deception. The storytellers move between everyday concerns and encounters with the supernatural without losing their appetite for a good plot.

The repetitions and stories within stories can make this first volume slow going in long stretches. Read in smaller portions, it is easier to enjoy the humour and ingenuity of the telling. The cruelty can be startling, as can the sudden changes of fortune. A rich collection, with plenty left to explore in the later volumes.
