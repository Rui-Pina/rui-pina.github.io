Kotkin follows Stalin through collectivization, famine and the purges, while keeping the approaching war with Germany in view. He connects decisions inside the Kremlin to their consequences across the Soviet Union. Stalin appears as a ruler who intervened in extraordinary detail and demanded obedience even as his own policies created fresh disasters.

The sections on diplomacy and the approach to the German invasion are compelling. Kotkin gives enough context to understand Stalin's calculations without excusing them. The sheer volume of names and administrative detail can be exhausting, but the breadth of the research makes this worth the effort. A strong account of how a dictator could accumulate such power and misjudge the danger facing him.
