Kotkin spends a substantial part of this biography on the Russian Empire and the revolutionaries who sought to destroy it. That breadth helps explain the opportunities Stalin exploited. His rise depended on civil war, party organization and the struggles among Bolshevik leaders, as well as his own ambition and ability to build power through appointments.

The attention to ideology is one of the book's strengths. Kotkin examines Stalin's political convictions and how he acted on them. The wider history can make for slow reading, but it gives his eventual dominance more context than a biography confined to personal character would. Dense, demanding and a strong foundation for the second volume.
