Andersen gives loneliness and longing an unusual place in fairy tales. His characters want love, recognition or a place among others, and he allows them to suffer without promising a comfortable ending. The Little Mermaid and The Little Match Girl are painful examples. Alongside that sadness, he has a sharp eye for vanity and the absurd things people do to preserve their status.

Across a complete collection, some of the moral lessons grow repetitive, and a few stories lean too hard on sentiment. At his best, though, Andersen writes with tenderness and a willingness to leave the reader unsettled. These stories deserve attention beyond the familiar childhood versions.
